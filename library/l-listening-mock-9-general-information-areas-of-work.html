Part 1. You will hear a telephone conversation between the manager of a shop run for charity and a young woman who is interested in volunteering. First, you have some time to look at questions 1 to 4. Now listen carefully and answer questions 1 to 4.

Mark: Good morning. Charity Links, Mark speaking.

Joanne: Oh, good morning. My name’s Joanne Richardson and I’m ringing about the advert I saw in your city centre shop window.

Mark: Asking for part-time volunteers to help in our shops?

Joanne: That’s right. Well, I’m interested in helping and wonder if I could ask a few questions.

Mark: Of course.

Joanne: I understand your charity helps local people with housing problems, is that right?

Mark: Yes, though it now focuses exclusively on helping the 1homeless.

Joanne: Oh, right. And I was just wondering, I live in the north of the city, do you have any shops in this area?

Mark: Yes, we have one on Roslyn Road.

Part 3 – Preparing a speech on Shakespeare

Now turn to Part 3. Part 3. You are going to hear a conversation between a student and a tutor. First, you have some time to look at questions 21 to 25. Now listen to the tape and answer questions 21 to 25.

Jeff: Hi Brad. I was wondering if you have time to answer some of my questions about my upcoming test.

Brad: Sure, no problem, Jeff. What is it that you are having problems on?

Jeff: Well, it’s for my English final. We have to prepare a five-minute speech to present in front of the whole class, including the professor. So I’m a little bit worried.

Brad: Is there any specific topic or can you do it on whatever you want?

Jeff: It has to have something to do with the origins of English literature. I’m thinking of doing it on Shakespeare, but 21I bet many other students will have the same idea.

Brad: That’s fine. Don’t worry if others are doing the same thing. As long as you do a good job, that’s all that counts. 22A good professor will grade all students fairly.

Jeff: You really think so?

Brad: I suppose Shakespeare is the most famous author, so it should be fine. Besides, Shakespeare has so many works, you only have to choose a couple of them and talk about those.

Jeff: I guess you’re right. Do you have any advice about how to prepare a speech?

Brad: First, you need to 23select your topic. Have you done this yet?

Jeff: Yes, I have lots of information on Shakespeare.

Brad: Good. Next, you should 24do a research on a specific topic. Do you have a deadline for which to turn in your speech topic?

Jeff: The deadline is next Tuesday.

Brad: So you should have a detailed outline of what you will say by then. Do not just turn in a piece of paper saying Shakespeare on it. That will not give your professor any ideas to what you will be talking about.

Jeff: OK. So you think I should write out an outline of my speech?

Brad: Of course! Writing your speech out in outline form is essential. No one could give a speech from scratch. Even the president must refer to his outline when giving a speech. 25An outline will give you a good structure to base your speech on.

Now look at questions 26 to 30. Now listen to the tape and answer questions 26 to 30.

Jeff: That’s true. I was thinking that I would do an outline last, after I figured everything out. But I think your idea is better. What should I do after I have an outline prepared?

Brad: You should then write the speech out, word for word, what you’re going to say. This is so 26you’ll have a firm idea of what you will say. It doesn’t mean that the speech you will give will be exactly the same, but at least you have a fairly good idea what the final product will be. At this point, I can read it over for you if you want.

Jeff: Really? That would be great! I would appreciate that so much!

Brad: No problem. Once you write it out, the next step is to practise giving the speech. At first, you can do it 27in front of the mirror, so you can see your expressions in your presentation. After that, you should practise giving your speech 28to some friends. I can listen to it for you, too.

Part 4 – Case study on LCP Technologies

Part 4. First, you have some time to look at questions 31 to 40. Now listen carefully and answer questions 31 to 40.

Lecturer: The basis of my work has been a case study of a company in India called LCP Technologies. You may have heard about it as it has been in the news lately. India, as you know, is famous for its high quality software companies. LCP Technologies is a software company specialising in outsourcing, designing solutions to specific problems other companies have. And it’s one of the biggest by size in the country with over 10,000 employees. The new CEO, Manjeet Khanna, has built a reputation for innovation that has attracted attention from every continent. His basic idea is not to reduce levels of management but to make the workplace more 31democratic. By that I don’t mean that everyone has equal power and equal responsibility, which most people would agree would make a company impossible to manage. But everyone is encouraged to be a critical and active part of the company. Let me give you an example.

Many companies have a system of rating and grading employees and managers are also graded, but normally by superiors as this sort of information generally goes upwards and it’s used for reviews and performance assessments. In LCP Technologies, everyone, including the CEO, is graded. And what’s more, these grades are published on the internal staff 32website, accessible for everyone in the company. The initial reaction from managers not used to such a concept of 33transparency was understandably negative, as you can imagine, but it did work. According to Khanna, one benefit in particular is that the culture has changed. It’s now one of openness, much more so than in the past. What’s more, managers and in fact the whole workforce now feel much more comfortable commenting on and also correcting any management faults. So, the philosophy has encouraged good communication at all levels of the company and that works to the benefit of everyone.

In fact, the motto Khanna goes by, which may sound unusual in an era where we are all used to thinking of the customer as king, is 34employee first. The benefits of this style are obvious. One of LCP’s problems was high staff turnover, with trained software engineers leaving to join another company, sometimes after just a few months’ service. This is one of the typical problems of industries that are in demand. However, the company now manages to keep its staff. And according to statistics, considerably fewer key engineers are leaving than the industry average, indicating a high level of job satisfaction. This has been an important part of enabling the terrific growth of the company and that, in turn, has seen 35income increase by 32% in two years, which is pretty astonishing.

What’s more, according to Khanna, his philosophy reinforces the message that the 36solution to a problem can come from any part of the company, not simply from the top. The system depends on winning the trust of everyone involved, of course. Khanna again broke with tradition by making it clear that grading would not be used to determine 37promotion.

Two other tools that form part of his management style are his personal touch. He answers all emails from anyone in the company in person and is on record as saying that this can take up to 10 hours a week of his time. And finally, he introduced a novel approach to dealing with staff issues. Khanna developed an online forum for complaints. Each one is called a 38ticket, since that avoids the overtly unpleasant image of a complaint. Any employee or manager can post one online about absolutely anything, whether about faulty air conditioning, food choices in the cafeteria, or even much more sensitive issues such as the length of 39vacation you’re allowed. When the new system for dealing with complaints was introduced, people were unsure about criticising the company and so sent their complaints in anonymously. But then it was impossible to check if they were happy with the action taken. So, a 40ban was placed on this, and now everyone has to take responsibility for their complaint. Another interesting aspect of the company…

That is the end of Part 4. You now have one minute to check your answers to Part 4. That is the end of the listening test.
