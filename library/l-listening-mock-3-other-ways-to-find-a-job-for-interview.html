Adviser: As a matter of fact, you’re in luck. They called this morning.

Sophia: That’d be great, as long as it’s in the 1factory. I don’t want to work in the warehouse.

Adviser: Yes, it is. They’re looking for machine operators at the moment.

Sophia: You don’t need experience for that, do you?

Adviser: No, it’s really very simple. They teach you everything you need to know.

Sophia: Oh, OK. What are the hours on that one? Most of my lectures finish by 5.30 at the latest.

Sophia: That’d be OK. I’d really like to go for that.

Adviser: Great. Let me give you the details. The job reference. It’s important to quote this when you contact them, as they have several vacancies at any one time, so it saves confusion and time. Sophia: Right, I will, thanks. Anything else?

Adviser: Let me see. Hmm. This might interest you. It’s in Cottons, the 3supermarket at the end of the high street.

Sophia: Oh yes, I know it. I go there sometimes.

Part 2 – Beach Clean-up Day

Green marks show where each answer is heard.

Hi everybody, thank you very much for agreeing to participate in the next beach clean-up day. These days are a real benefit for our community. I’m Brenda, the organiser, and I just want to explain a bit about the day to help you prepare and to answer any questions you might have.

The beach that’s been chosen for the clean-up day is Quinlan Beach. Because it’s easily accessible by public transport, the beach is really popular and well used by locals and tourists alike. The town council does its best to provide bins, but litter is still a problem. It’s important to deal with this as 11it’s a threat to the groups of seals that swim around there and regularly rest on the flat rocks. When you observe them in their natural habitat, you really sense why you’re picking up this litter.

You’ll be working in groups of three to four people at the beach. Some of you have signed up as groups already, which is great. The rest of you will be organised into groups on the day. There are different roles in the group. 12It’ll be up to you to choose who does what in the group. After lunch, we’ll form new groups which will give you a chance to get to know new people.

You may be wondering what kind of equipment you’ll need. You may have seen tools to pick up litter with. They’re like poles with a sharp end. 13If you want to use one of those, you’ll have to bring your own. After the safety briefing, we’ll give you each a whistle to blow in case you need to get our attention. The town council is providing special collection bags for us to use, which are fully biodegradable.

There are a few things to keep in mind regarding clothing for the day. Definitely go for something comfortable and 14wear shoes or boots that will keep your feet dry. We’re all going to be given yellow caps so we can identify each other. The town council is supplying vinyl gloves, but they’re quite large, so your own may be more comfortable. Sunscreen is up to you, but highly recommended.

Each group that goes out picking up litter will be given a survey to fill in as you go along. The town council can get an estimate of the quantity and the different kinds of litter we gather up by examining the bags we collect. However, 15they are very keen to know more about where you find the rubbish, which may help them in future. So that’s what you’re asked to indicate on the survey.

Okay, now please look at the map showing Quinlan Beach. When you arrive, you can park in the car park, which is at the bottom of the map.

16The first aid station is close to the shore and will be staffed all day in case anyone needs it. You can find it by taking the footpath that goes north from the car park towards the beach. The path branches out, but carry on straight and there it is.

To get to the 17equipment pickup, walk along the road that leads from the car park around the bend and past the bus stop. There’s a small building before you get to the golf club and that’s where the equipment is kept.

You’ll want to know where the toilets are. 18They’re adjacent to the car park on the opposite side from where the wooded area is.

You can see the beach is sort of divided into two stretches by Rocky Point. 19The meeting point for the beach clean-up is on the beach that’s closest to the cafe.

Later in the day, you’ll move to the West Beach. We’ll be meeting for lunch at 1pm. Some food is available in the cafe or you can bring your own. 20We’ll meet in the area immediately outside the entrance to the Grand Pier where there are picnic tables. You get a good view from there.

So now, if there are any questions, then…

Part 3 – Action Research Course for Practising Teachers

Green marks show where each answer is heard.

Student: Dr. Williams.

Dr. Williams: Come in.

Student: Thanks.

Dr. Williams: How can I help you?

Student: Well, I’m interested in doing the course on action research.

Dr. Williams: Sure. What would you like to know?

Student: Well, first of all, I understand it’s about ways of researching your own practice in your professional role so as to improve it.

Dr. Williams: That’s right. And I’m afraid I must emphasise at the outset that it’s only open to working teachers.

Student: Well, I’ve been one for eight years, so…

Dr. Williams: That’s plenty and…

Student: And can I ask, will you be teaching on it?

Dr. Williams: Yes, I’ve done so for many years, but the course leader will be one of our new lecturers, Alan Cumner.

Student: Oh.

Dr. Williams: He’s just written a very interesting book on action research, which you should get hold of. The title is 21Professional Learning.

Student: Right. I think one of the other teachers in my school mentioned it. OK. Can I ask you what will be covered on the course?

Dr. Williams: Well, the obvious aim is to develop skills in using a variety of research techniques.

Student: Yes, of course. I’ll ask you about those in more detail later.

Dr. Williams: And then there’s something that’s crucial to action research, and that’s the skill of researching in a 22team.

Student: Yes, I can say that it is very important. And what about after you’ve done the research? Do you do something on dissemination of findings?

Dr. Williams: Well, the last major objective is similar to that, but not quite the same. It’s on 23presenting results as part of the wider collaborative culture.

Student: Great. Well, that’s very helpful. I can ask you about the different components.

Dr. Williams: Well, the course is organised around the data gathering techniques, and we divide them into two main types, observational and non-observational techniques. And between each session, we ask you to try out the technique for yourself in a variety of settings. The initial sessions are on using observation checklists.

Student: Oh, yes.

Dr. Williams: And we ask you to try some out in a school, so you can look at an element of a lesson, such as pupil 24behaviour, so you can immediately apply what you’ve learnt to a real situation in the classroom.

Student: Right. Somebody told me we have to keep a 25diary.

Dr. Williams: Yes, that’s right. We ask you to keep one as a research tool.

Student: And what’s the point of that exactly?

Dr. Williams: Well, it really enhances your capacity for reflection.

Student: Oh, that sounds interesting.

Dr. Williams: And then we’ve just added another observational technique to the course, which is 26video recording.

Student: I have tried that already with colleagues. I have to say, with only limited success.

Dr. Williams: Yes, that’s been our experience too, especially at the beginning. So we decided to get you to try this technique out with your fellow students in classroom 27simulation, which I think you’ll agree…

Student: That has fewer problems, yes.

Dr. Williams: And then we have sessions examining what we call non-observational techniques. And rather controversially, we start with ways of scrutinising 28test results.

Student: Oh, right. Yeah, I’m familiar with that.

Dr. Williams: Yes, but we think these can yield some very valuable insights. We ask you to bring along statistics relating to your own students for this.

Student: Oh, that should be easy. I have to say, I quite enjoy that kind of analysis.

Dr. Williams: Yes. And then we look at using questionnaires.

Student: Oh, yes. I’ve done one or two projects involving those. I used one local teachers resource group, but I’ve never felt that I’ve used them as effectively as I should have done.

Dr. Williams: Well, that is a common comment from our participants. And we thought we’d open things up a bit this year with a different approach and use the 29internet in order to identify respondents. Obviously, we’d explain more about how that will work closer to the time.

Student: Great. Look forward to that. And will there be anything on 30interviews?

Dr. Williams: That’s really the last major section, and we ask you to choose your own respondents for that as part of a final project.

Student: Great. Well, that’s been really helpful, Dr Williams. Thank you.

Part 4 – Diploma Course in Food Technology

Green marks show where each answer is heard.

It’s my pleasure to welcome all new students to our food technology programme. Our diploma course equips you with the know-how and skills to take part in every aspect of food production. And in fact we now go beyond production, and for the first time this year we’re also including a module on 31distribution, so that the whole chain is covered.

This will give you an even more comprehensive understanding of the industry. You’ll work not only on established food lines, but you’ll also be able to participate in the development of new foods. However, throughout the course the emphasis is first and foremost on food 32safety.

This is an area the industry is tightening up on. I should also mention that you are able to choose an extra option, that is 33storage. Many students find that they’re interested in taking this add-on, as it gives them more time to go into this very important area.

It means more hours of lectures, but I’m sure you’ll find it very worthwhile. Now it may seem premature, but I would like to mention career opportunities, because there has been quite a shift in recent years. Previously, many of our graduates typically became food production managers, but now both government bodies and private agencies are in need of 34inspectors, to see that exacting standards are being met.

So that’s something you may wish to bear in mind as you go through the course. Now, going back to the course itself, what are the most useful subjects to have studied before you begin? Well, many students come to us with a strong background in 35chemistry and physics, though it’s actually the former that most students feel gives them an edge in the first year. It’s a building block, if you like.

So, let me give you a brief overview of the three-year course. In Year 1, you develop your understanding of the core scientific disciplines underpinning food technology, including biology and maths. In Year 2, you go into more detail.

This is where we begin to look at real-world applications of what you studied in Year 1. In particular, we focus on food 36processing, which involves studying the properties of raw materials. This will be supported by practical experiments in our small-scale production facility. Year 2 also covers the theoretical aspects of dehydration, one of the most commonly used methods of preserving foods.

You’ll also look at 37packaging and how it can keep products such as coffee and salad fresh. Nowadays, new regulations demand we inform people about exactly what’s in every food product. So, we devote time on the course to studying 38labels.

In Year 3, you are encouraged to develop further your particular interests and expertise in food technology through a research project. Recent projects have included an investigation into the effects of heat treatment on acidified dairy products and combined heat and pressure treatment on 39proteins. We will support you in your choice of project as far as possible.

Before I finish, I’d just like to say a few words about our industry partners. We wouldn’t be able to offer such dynamic courses if it weren’t for several large companies. Together, they provide us with the backing to finance 40research and this has allowed us to invest our own funds in new buildings and machinery for the college.

I believe that I can also say that our alliance is of mutual benefit in that some of the ideas that our students have worked on have been taken up by several of our industry partners. Thank you.
