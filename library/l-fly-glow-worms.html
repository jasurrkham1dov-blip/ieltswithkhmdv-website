Today we are going to dive into the fascinating world of a special kind of firefly. These glow worms are particularly interesting because of their unique way of living and surviving. One of the intriguing aspects of these glow-worms is that only fly worm larvae can eat. Once they become adults, they can no longer feed, which is why they have such a short lifespan, just about 14 days.

The light produced by these larvae is seen as warnings by other creatures, particularly the predators. This makes their survival even more interesting.

Adults, also known as fly worms, tend to stay in the same area and build colonies of glow worms. This can provide us with insights into their social behaviour as well. When the larvae are hungry, they tend to glow brighter, and that differentiates them from other glow worms around them.

Interestingly, many fireflies show a lot of activity during the day, although this might be contrary to popular belief. It highlights their varied behaviour patterns.

Speaking of behaviour, the term 'arachno' refers to how glow-worms catch flying insects, similar to how spiders do.

The research conducted on these glow-worms has provided us with genetic information. This includes biochemical studies on how the glowworm produces light, and crucial data aiding future ecological and evolutionary studies of these fascinating creatures.
